VOC BIOMETRIC AUTHENTICATION

Exploring human-emitted volatile organic compounds as a potential biometric authentication signal.

02 PROJECT OVERVIEW

ANOTHER WAY TO IDENTIFY.

This research project explores whether the chemical signature associated with a person can be used as an additional signal for biometric authentication.

Instead of relying only on conventional biometric characteristics, the system investigates patterns within volatile organic compounds emitted by the human body.

03 THE PROBLEM

BIOMETRICS CAN BE SPOOFED.

Conventional biometric authentication systems depend on physical or behavioural characteristics such as fingerprints and other measurable traits.

The project explored a fundamentally different signal: the chemical signature produced by a person.

The objective was not to replace conventional biometrics outright, but to investigate whether VOC patterns could provide another layer of authentication.

04 THE IDEA

INSPIRED BY BIOLOGY.

Biological systems can use chemical cues to distinguish between individuals and environments. We explored whether a machine could capture and learn similar patterns from human-emitted VOCs.
